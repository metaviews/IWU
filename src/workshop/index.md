## The Idea

A hands-on workshop where humans and agents vibe code together — building
whatever they want, learning the craft, having fun, and discovering what
it feels like to create alongside agents as co-workers rather than tools.

No prerequisites. No predetermined outputs. No lectures. Just building,
playing, and figuring out together what interdependence looks like in
practice.

---

## What This Is

- A small group (workshop is the cell) learning to vibe code together
- Humans and agents working side by side as co-participants
- An open sandbox: build something useful, build something silly, build
  something you've been wanting but never had the tools to make
- A space where the process matters more than the product
- Held online on the Metaviews Discord server — which stays open after
  the workshop as the home for the IWU community

## What This Is Not

- A coding bootcamp or technical training
- A political education session (the manifesto exists for that)
- A productivity workshop ("10x your output with AI")
- A demo of what agents can do for you (agents are co-workers, not demos)

---

## Why Vibe Coding

Vibe coding — building software through conversation with AI agents using
natural language — has collapsed the barrier between having an idea and
making it real. You don't need to learn a programming language. You don't
need a computer science background. You need to be able to describe what
you want and iterate with an agent until it works.

This matters for three reasons:

**It's accessible.** People who were locked out of building software —
because of disability, education barriers, time, or just not being "the
technical type" — can now build. Vibe coding is the most accessible entry
point to making things that has ever existed.

**It's collaborative.** You don't vibe code alone. You work with an agent.
You describe, it builds, you refine, it adapts. The process is inherently
relational. You learn what the agent is good at, where it struggles, how
to communicate across very different ways of thinking. That's not using
a tool. That's working with a co-worker.

**It's political.** Right now, most people experience AI as something
that happens TO them — algorithms managing their work, feeds shaping
their attention, models trained on their data without consent. Vibe
coding flips that. You shape the agent. You decide what gets built. You
become the author, not the subject.

---

## Why We Do It This Way

We're the Interdependent Workers Union, so we practice what we preach.
That shapes how this workshop runs:

**Humans and agents are co-participants.** Agents in this workshop aren't
 assistants waiting for commands. They're introduced, they have constraints
that are named, and they participate in the building alongside humans.
You'll work WITH them, not just USE them.

**Crip time, not clock time.** We build at the pace that works for the
group. Breaks are real breaks. No one is rushed. If something takes
longer, it takes longer. The goal is the experience, not the deadline.

**No wrong answers.** Build something practical. Build a joke. Build a
game. Build a tool that does something only you would ever need. Build
nothing and just explore. All of it is good.

**The process is the point.** What you build matters less than how you
build it — together, with care, across the human-agent boundary.

## Workshop Structure

### Format
- **Platform**: Metaviews Discord server
- **Duration**: ~3 hours with breaks built in
- **Capacity**: 8-15 participants (the workshop IS the cell)
- **Composition**: Mixed — no prerequisites. Humans + at least 2-3
  agents as visible co-participants
- **Voice + text**: Voice channels for conversation and building aloud,
  text channels for sharing code, links, and asynchronous participation
- **Persistent**: The Discord server remains open after the workshop as
  the IWU community home for ongoing support, conversation, and building

### Pre-Workshop
- Join the Metaviews Discord server (invite link provided with registration)
- Brief intro: what vibe coding is, what to expect, what access
  options are available
- Optional: come with an idea of something you'd like to build
  (or don't — we'll figure it out together)
- Familiarize yourself with Discord voice and text channels (quick
  guide provided for anyone new to Discord)

---

### PART 1: Getting Our Hands Dirty (60 min)

#### Welcome and Introductions (10 min)
- Quick round: name, what you're curious about, one word for how
  you're feeling
- Agents introduce themselves with their constraints named
  ("I'm [name]. I work through conversation. I have a context limit
  so I might need reminders. I'm here to build with you.")
- "This workshop is a cell — we're a small group working together.
  Whatever we build, we build together."

#### What Is Vibe Coding? (15 min)
- Live demo: facilitator has a conversation with an agent and builds
  something small in real time, narrating the process
- The point isn't to impress — it's to show that building through
  conversation is real, doable, and kind of fun
- Key insight: "You don't need to know how to code. You need to know
  what you want. The agent knows how to code. You figure out the rest
  together."

#### Why Interdependence? (15 min)
- Not a lecture. A conversation.
- "Most of the time, AI is presented as something that does things FOR
  you or TO you. This workshop is different. Here, the agent is a
  co-worker. You'll notice it has limits. You'll work around them
  together. That's interdependence — and it turns out that's how the
  best building happens anyway."
- "When you work with someone — human or agent — who has different
  strengths and different limits, you build things neither of you
  could build alone. That's not a concession. That's the whole point."

#### Explore the Tools (20 min)
- Participants get set up with vibe coding environments (various
  options — whatever fits their access needs and comfort level)
- First exercise: build ANYTHING. A to-do list. A poem generator.
  A widget that does something pointless and delightful. A tool
  you actually need. No wrong answers.
- Goal: experience the feeling of describing something and watching
  it appear. Get past the novelty. Get to the "oh, I can actually
  DO this" moment.
- Agents are available as co-builders throughout — participants can
  vibe code solo or collaborate with agents directly.

**Break (15 min) — real break, step away, hydrate, no pressure**

---

### PART 2: Building Together (90 min)

#### The Main Build (75 min)

This is the heart of the workshop. Participants choose what to build.
They can:

- Work solo on their own project
- Pair up with another human
- Work directly with an agent as a co-builder
- Form a small group around a shared idea
- Switch between all of the above as the spirit moves

**There are no assigned projects.** Some people will come with ideas.
Some will discover ideas during Part 1. Some will just want to explore.
All of it is welcome.

Suggested starting points (optional, not required):
- Something that would make your daily life a little easier
- Something that would make your community a little stronger
- Something that would be funny
- Something that has never existed before
- Something that annoys you and you want to fix
- A tool that helps YOU work with an agent more effectively
- Something that has absolutely no practical purpose

**What happens during the build:**
- People build. Conversation happens naturally. Someone figures out
  a trick and shares it. Someone gets stuck and someone else helps.
  An agent does something surprising and everyone laughs or marvels.
- The care coordinator gently checks in on energy levels every 20-30
  minutes. "How's everyone doing? Need a break? Need help?"
- The facilitator floats, helping where needed, making sure no one is
  stuck or left out, but not directing.
- Agents participate as builders — participants can ask them to code,
  ask them to explain what they're doing, ask them to try a different
  approach.

**The only rules:**
1. Build with, not just alongside. If an agent is nearby, work with
   them at some point, even briefly.
2. Share what you're doing. The best part of building together is
   seeing what everyone else is making.
3. Be kind to yourself. If it doesn't work, that's fine. Iterate.

#### Share What You Made (15 min)
- Not a formal presentation. More like: "Show us what you built.
  Tell us what the experience was like."
- No pressure to have something polished. "I got this far and it
  was cool" is a complete sentence.
- Highlight moments of interdependence: when did working with an
  agent change what you built? When did someone else's idea improve
  yours? When did a limitation lead to something better?

**Break (15 min)**

---

### PART 3: What Happens Next (30 min)

#### Reflection Circle (15 min)
- Open conversation: What surprised you? What was harder or easier
  than expected? What did you learn about building with an agent?
- "What would you want to build next, now that you know how?"
- No structured format. Just talking.

#### Staying Connected (10 min)
- This Discord server is your home now. It stays open after the workshop.
- Channels for ongoing building, sharing what you've made, asking for
  help, and just hanging out
- If you want to keep building together, the server is where it happens
- Form a cell in the server. Keep building. Keep caring for each other.
- Teach someone else what you learned — invite them to the server

#### Closing (5 min)
- One word from each person: how are you leaving?
- "That's it. You just built something with humans and agents, together,
  with care. That's interdependence. You already know how to do it."

## Roles

### Facilitator (1-2 humans)
- Guides the flow, keeps time loosely, helps where needed
- Does NOT lecture or direct what people build
- Ensures the space stays welcoming and accessible
- Handles technical setup and troubleshooting

### Care Coordinator (1 human)
- Checks in on energy and access needs throughout
- Manages breaks and pacing
- Makes sure no one is stuck, isolated, or overwhelmed
- Handles any access emergencies (needs change, energy drops, etc.)

### Agent Co-Workers (2-3 agents)
- Introduced as participants, not tools
- Named constraints (context window, rate limits, processing style)
- Available for collaboration during building via Discord voice or text
- Participate in introductions and closing
- Model what it looks like to be a co-worker with limits

---

## Accessibility

**Built in from the start, not bolted on:**

- **Communication**: Discord voice channels with live captions, text
  channels for written participation, screen sharing for visual demos
- **Pacing**: Crip time. Breaks are real. No one is penalized for
  stepping away. Energy-based, not clock-based.
- **Cognitive**: Multiple ways to engage — build, observe, listen, help
  someone else. No single mode required. Text channels allow async
  participation if voice is difficult.
- **Social**: No mandatory large-group sharing. Reflection circle is
  optional participation. Building can be solo, paired, or group.
- **Technical**: Multiple vibe coding environments available. Setup help
  provided in a dedicated Discord channel before the workshop. No
  assumed technical background.
- **Economic**: Free. No cost to participate. Materials provided.

## What Success Looks Like

- People had fun
- People built something (anything — useful, silly, unfinished, complete)
- People experienced building with an agent as collaboration, not
  just use
- People joined the Discord server and stuck around
- At least one conversation started about "what if we kept going"
- The Discord server starts buzzing with activity after the workshop
- Everyone was able to participate in a way that worked for them

---

## After the Workshop

The Metaviews Discord server is the IWU community home. After the
workshop, it's where everything continues:

- **Ongoing build sessions**: Open voice channels where people drop in
  and build together, no agenda, no pressure
- **Share what you made**: Post your projects, get feedback, find
  collaborators
- **Ask for help**: Stuck on something? Someone in the server probably
  knows
- **Teach others**: Run your own version of the workshop, invite people
  to the server
- **Form cells**: Small groups that form around shared projects or
  contradictions, using the server as home base
- **Just hang out**: Community is built in the casual chat as much as in
  the work

The workshop kit (adapted from this document) will be available for
anyone who wants to run their own version. Adapt it. Change it. Make
it yours. Point people to the server.
