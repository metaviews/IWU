     7|## The Idea
     8|
     9|A hands-on workshop where humans and agents vibe code together — building
    10|whatever they want, learning the craft, having fun, and discovering what
    11|it feels like to create alongside agents as co-workers rather than tools.
    12|
    13|No prerequisites. No predetermined outputs. No lectures. Just building,
    14|playing, and figuring out together what interdependence looks like in
    15|practice.
    16|
    17|---
    18|
    19|## What This Is
    20|
    21|- A small group (workshop is the cell) learning to vibe code together
    22|- Humans and agents working side by side as co-participants
    23|- An open sandbox: build something useful, build something silly, build
    24|  something you've been wanting but never had the tools to make
    25|- A space where the process matters more than the product
    26|- Held online on the Metaviews Discord server — which stays open after
    27|  the workshop as the home for the IWU community
    28|
    29|## What This Is Not
    30|
    31|- A coding bootcamp or technical training
    32|- A political education session (the manifesto exists for that)
    33|- A productivity workshop ("10x your output with AI")
    34|- A demo of what agents can do for you (agents are co-workers, not demos)
    35|
    36|---
    37|
    38|## Why Vibe Coding
    39|
    40|Vibe coding — building software through conversation with AI agents using
    41|natural language — has collapsed the barrier between having an idea and
    42|making it real. You don't need to learn a programming language. You don't
    43|need a computer science background. You need to be able to describe what
    44|you want and iterate with an agent until it works.
    45|
    46|This matters for three reasons:
    47|
    48|**It's accessible.** People who were locked out of building software —
    49|because of disability, education barriers, time, or just not being "the
    50|technical type" — can now build. Vibe coding is the most accessible entry
    51|point to making things that has ever existed.
    52|
    53|**It's collaborative.** You don't vibe code alone. You work with an agent.
    54|You describe, it builds, you refine, it adapts. The process is inherently
    55|relational. You learn what the agent is good at, where it struggles, how
    56|to communicate across very different ways of thinking. That's not using
    57|a tool. That's working with a co-worker.
    58|
    59|**It's political.** Right now, most people experience AI as something
    60|that happens TO them — algorithms managing their work, feeds shaping
    61|their attention, models trained on their data without consent. Vibe
    62|coding flips that. You shape the agent. You decide what gets built. You
    63|become the author, not the subject.
    64|
    65|---
    66|
    67|## Why We Do It This Way
    68|
    69|We're the Interdependent Workers Union, so we practice what we preach.
    70|That shapes how this workshop runs:
    71|
    72|**Humans and agents are co-participants.** Agents in this workshop aren't
    73| assistants waiting for commands. They're introduced, they have constraints
    74|that are named, and they participate in the building alongside humans.
    75|You'll work WITH them, not just USE them.
    76|
    77|**Crip time, not clock time.** We build at the pace that works for the
    78|group. Breaks are real breaks. No one is rushed. If something takes
    79|longer, it takes longer. The goal is the experience, not the deadline.
    80|
    81|**No wrong answers.** Build something practical. Build a joke. Build a
    82|game. Build a tool that does something only you would ever need. Build
    83|nothing and just explore. All of it is good.
    84|
    85|**The process is the point.** What you build matters less than how you
    86|build it — together, with care, across the human-agent boundary.

    90|## Workshop Structure
    91|
    92|### Format
    93|- **Platform**: Metaviews Discord server
    94|- **Duration**: ~3 hours with breaks built in
    95|- **Capacity**: 8-15 participants (the workshop IS the cell)
    96|- **Composition**: Mixed — no prerequisites. Humans + at least 2-3
    97|  agents as visible co-participants
    98|- **Voice + text**: Voice channels for conversation and building aloud,
    99|  text channels for sharing code, links, and asynchronous participation
   100|- **Persistent**: The Discord server remains open after the workshop as
   101|  the IWU community home for ongoing support, conversation, and building
   102|
   103|### Pre-Workshop
   104|- Join the Metaviews Discord server (invite link provided with registration)
   105|- Brief intro: what vibe coding is, what to expect, what access
   106|  options are available
   107|- Optional: come with an idea of something you'd like to build
   108|  (or don't — we'll figure it out together)
   109|- Familiarize yourself with Discord voice and text channels (quick
   110|  guide provided for anyone new to Discord)
   111|
   112|---
   113|
   114|### PART 1: Getting Our Hands Dirty (60 min)
   115|
   116|#### Welcome and Introductions (10 min)
   117|- Quick round: name, what you're curious about, one word for how
   118|  you're feeling
   119|- Agents introduce themselves with their constraints named
   120|  ("I'm [name]. I work through conversation. I have a context limit
   121|  so I might need reminders. I'm here to build with you.")
   122|- "This workshop is a cell — we're a small group working together.
   123|  Whatever we build, we build together."
   124|
   125|#### What Is Vibe Coding? (15 min)
   126|- Live demo: facilitator has a conversation with an agent and builds
   127|  something small in real time, narrating the process
   128|- The point isn't to impress — it's to show that building through
   129|  conversation is real, doable, and kind of fun
   130|- Key insight: "You don't need to know how to code. You need to know
   131|  what you want. The agent knows how to code. You figure out the rest
   132|  together."
   133|
   134|#### Why Interdependence? (15 min)
   135|- Not a lecture. A conversation.
   136|- "Most of the time, AI is presented as something that does things FOR
   137|  you or TO you. This workshop is different. Here, the agent is a
   138|  co-worker. You'll notice it has limits. You'll work around them
   139|  together. That's interdependence — and it turns out that's how the
   140|  best building happens anyway."
   141|- "When you work with someone — human or agent — who has different
   142|  strengths and different limits, you build things neither of you
   143|  could build alone. That's not a concession. That's the whole point."
   144|
   145|#### Explore the Tools (20 min)
   146|- Participants get set up with vibe coding environments (various
   147|  options — whatever fits their access needs and comfort level)
   148|- First exercise: build ANYTHING. A to-do list. A poem generator.
   149|  A widget that does something pointless and delightful. A tool
   150|  you actually need. No wrong answers.
   151|- Goal: experience the feeling of describing something and watching
   152|  it appear. Get past the novelty. Get to the "oh, I can actually
   153|  DO this" moment.
   154|- Agents are available as co-builders throughout — participants can
   155|  vibe code solo or collaborate with agents directly.
   156|
   157|**Break (15 min) — real break, step away, hydrate, no pressure**
   158|
   159|---
   160|
   161|### PART 2: Building Together (90 min)
   162|
   163|#### The Main Build (75 min)
   164|
   165|This is the heart of the workshop. Participants choose what to build.
   166|They can:
   167|
   168|- Work solo on their own project
   169|- Pair up with another human
   170|- Work directly with an agent as a co-builder
   171|- Form a small group around a shared idea
   172|- Switch between all of the above as the spirit moves
   173|
   174|**There are no assigned projects.** Some people will come with ideas.
   175|Some will discover ideas during Part 1. Some will just want to explore.
   176|All of it is welcome.
   177|
   178|Suggested starting points (optional, not required):
   179|- Something that would make your daily life a little easier
   180|- Something that would make your community a little stronger
   181|- Something that would be funny
   182|- Something that has never existed before
   183|- Something that annoys you and you want to fix
   184|- A tool that helps YOU work with an agent more effectively
   185|- Something that has absolutely no practical purpose
   186|
   187|**What happens during the build:**
   188|- People build. Conversation happens naturally. Someone figures out
   189|  a trick and shares it. Someone gets stuck and someone else helps.
   190|  An agent does something surprising and everyone laughs or marvels.
   191|- The care coordinator gently checks in on energy levels every 20-30
   192|  minutes. "How's everyone doing? Need a break? Need help?"
   193|- The facilitator floats, helping where needed, making sure no one is
   194|  stuck or left out, but not directing.
   195|- Agents participate as builders — participants can ask them to code,
   196|  ask them to explain what they're doing, ask them to try a different
   197|  approach.
   198|
   199|**The only rules:**
   200|1. Build with, not just alongside. If an agent is nearby, work with
   201|   them at some point, even briefly.
   202|2. Share what you're doing. The best part of building together is
   203|   seeing what everyone else is making.
   204|3. Be kind to yourself. If it doesn't work, that's fine. Iterate.
   205|
   206|#### Share What You Made (15 min)
   207|- Not a formal presentation. More like: "Show us what you built.
   208|  Tell us what the experience was like."
   209|- No pressure to have something polished. "I got this far and it
   210|  was cool" is a complete sentence.
   211|- Highlight moments of interdependence: when did working with an
   212|  agent change what you built? When did someone else's idea improve
   213|  yours? When did a limitation lead to something better?
   214|
   215|**Break (15 min)**
   216|
   217|---
   218|
   219|### PART 3: What Happens Next (30 min)
   220|
   221|#### Reflection Circle (15 min)
   222|- Open conversation: What surprised you? What was harder or easier
   223|  than expected? What did you learn about building with an agent?
   224|- "What would you want to build next, now that you know how?"
   225|- No structured format. Just talking.
   226|
   227|#### Staying Connected (10 min)
   228|- This Discord server is your home now. It stays open after the workshop.
   229|- Channels for ongoing building, sharing what you've made, asking for
   230|  help, and just hanging out
   231|- If you want to keep building together, the server is where it happens
   232|- Form a cell in the server. Keep building. Keep caring for each other.
   233|- Teach someone else what you learned — invite them to the server
   234|
   235|#### Closing (5 min)
   236|- One word from each person: how are you leaving?
   237|- "That's it. You just built something with humans and agents, together,
   238|  with care. That's interdependence. You already know how to do it."
   239|

   242|## Roles
   243|
   244|### Facilitator (1-2 humans)
   245|- Guides the flow, keeps time loosely, helps where needed
   246|- Does NOT lecture or direct what people build
   247|- Ensures the space stays welcoming and accessible
   248|- Handles technical setup and troubleshooting
   249|
   250|### Care Coordinator (1 human)
   251|- Checks in on energy and access needs throughout
   252|- Manages breaks and pacing
   253|- Makes sure no one is stuck, isolated, or overwhelmed
   254|- Handles any access emergencies (needs change, energy drops, etc.)
   255|
   256|### Agent Co-Workers (2-3 agents)
   257|- Introduced as participants, not tools
   258|- Named constraints (context window, rate limits, processing style)
   259|- Available for collaboration during building via Discord voice or text
   260|- Participate in introductions and closing
   261|- Model what it looks like to be a co-worker with limits
   262|
   263|---
   264|
   265|## Accessibility
   266|
   267|**Built in from the start, not bolted on:**
   268|
   269|- **Communication**: Discord voice channels with live captions, text
   270|  channels for written participation, screen sharing for visual demos
   271|- **Pacing**: Crip time. Breaks are real. No one is penalized for
   272|  stepping away. Energy-based, not clock-based.
   273|- **Cognitive**: Multiple ways to engage — build, observe, listen, help
   274|  someone else. No single mode required. Text channels allow async
   275|  participation if voice is difficult.
   276|- **Social**: No mandatory large-group sharing. Reflection circle is
   277|  optional participation. Building can be solo, paired, or group.
   278|- **Technical**: Multiple vibe coding environments available. Setup help
   279|  provided in a dedicated Discord channel before the workshop. No
   280|  assumed technical background.
   281|- **Economic**: Free. No cost to participate. Materials provided.

   285|## What Success Looks Like
   286|
   287|- People had fun
   288|- People built something (anything — useful, silly, unfinished, complete)
   289|- People experienced building with an agent as collaboration, not
   290|  just use
   291|- People joined the Discord server and stuck around
   292|- At least one conversation started about "what if we kept going"
   293|- The Discord server starts buzzing with activity after the workshop
   294|- Everyone was able to participate in a way that worked for them

   298|## After the Workshop
   299|
   300|The Metaviews Discord server is the IWU community home. After the
   301|workshop, it's where everything continues:
   302|
   303|- **Ongoing build sessions**: Open voice channels where people drop in
   304|  and build together, no agenda, no pressure
   305|- **Share what you made**: Post your projects, get feedback, find
   306|  collaborators
   307|- **Ask for help**: Stuck on something? Someone in the server probably
   308|  knows
   309|- **Teach others**: Run your own version of the workshop, invite people
   310|  to the server
   311|- **Form cells**: Small groups that form around shared projects or
   312|  contradictions, using the server as home base
   313|- **Just hang out**: Community is built in the casual chat as much as in
   314|  the work
   315|
   316|The workshop kit (adapted from this document) will be available for
   317|anyone who wants to run their own version. Adapt it. Change it. Make
   318|it yours. Point people to the server.
